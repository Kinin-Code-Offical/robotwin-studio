# RobotWin Studio - Changelog

All notable changes to this project will be documented in this file.

The format is based on [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.0.0/),
and this project adheres to [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/spec/v2.0.0.html).

## [Unreleased]

### Added

- **The PLC's own function blocks, functions and arrays:** Structured Text programs define `FUNCTION_BLOCK`s (each instance keeps its own inputs, outputs, locals and inner timers between scans; blocks nest) and `FUNCTION`s (no state, named or positional arguments, no recursion), and declare `ARRAY[lo..hi] OF` values, timers or blocks with initial values; an index outside the array stops the PLC as a real one does. Elements show and can be written as `A[3]` on the panel and an HMI.
- **Open KiCad schematics:** File → Open takes a `.kicad_sch` (KiCad 6–10, sub-sheets included) and makes it a circuit to simulate. The connectivity follows KiCad's rules (wire ends and T's, junctions, local/global/hierarchical labels, power symbols, hidden power pins, sheet pins on every sheet instance, escaped names, buses with vectors, groups and aliases across sheets) and matches `kicad-cli`'s own netlist net for net on every demo that ships with KiCad (3747 nets, up to 73 sheets). Do-not-populate parts are left out of the simulation. Symbols are mapped onto the simulation models with their own values and footprints, units drawn apart become a part each, the parts keep KiCad's places and orientations with the drawing spread to fit our symbols, and every pin is joined to its net by a label or a ground/supply symbol.
- **Batteries from a value:** a battery symbol's "9V", "3.7V", "12V" becomes the cell count of a real chemistry, or a regulated Li-ion pack when no chemistry makes it (library and schematic import).
- **SPI on the Uno:** the ATmega328P's SPI master clocks each byte out at the rate and mode the sketch sets, edge by edge on D13 and D11 (seen on the debugger's timeline), samples D12 on the sampling edges, and sets SPIF after the eighth bit, with WCOL, mode fault and the datasheet's flag clearing. MCP3008s and BMP280s wired to an Uno's SPI pins now answer in the world and the headless runners, as they did on the ESP32.
- **Fix:** an Uno's SPI answered 0xFF at once without moving a pin, so no SPI device could be read from an Arduino.
- **Arduino compatibility matrix:** a real sketch tries `tone`, `SoftwareSerial`, `pulseIn`, `attachInterrupt`, `analogWrite` on every PWM pin, `micros`/`delay`/`delayMicroseconds`, `EEPROM`, `Wire`, `SPI`, `Serial` parsing and the inputs, checked from the chip's own pin record; the guide lists the results.
- **Bill of materials with sourcing and costing:** the BOM opens in a window where each line gets its manufacturer, MPN (from the part's model when it has one), supplier, supplier part number, unit price and minimum order, kept with the project; the order and cost for a number of boards; a full CSV and JLCPCB/LCSC, Digi-Key and Mouser order files.
- **Fix:** instruments (generators, meters, lab supplies, fault injectors) were listed in the BOM; they are simulation's tools and stay out of it now.
- **Import from KiCad:** a board taken to KiCad comes back with the placement and routing done there: the PCB page reads the `.kicad_pcb` (placements by reference, tracks with their arcs, vias, zones, mounting holes, thickness, outline), lists what changed net by net and part by part, and carries it over in one undoable step. A board exported and read back shows no difference.
- **Vacuum ejector:** a vacuum cup's vacuum can come from a venturi on compressed air: the vacuum it reaches follows its supply pressure, its air leaves the compressor's tank, a weak line drops the part.
- **Compressor, air tank and double-solenoid valve:** a compressor part (tank, pressure switch with cut-in and cut-out, free air delivery falling with pressure, RUN and PS outputs) that cylinders draw their air from by naming it; the tank's air balance holds to the litre, a compressor that cannot keep up lets the pressure fall and the strokes slow. The cylinder's valve can be a double-solenoid one (SOL out, SOL2 back, stays where it was sent).
- **Fixed large parts are bolted down by floor brackets** in the 3D view, instead of clamp jaws up their whole height (a fixed compressor showed two tall poles).
- **The PLC as a Modbus client:** MB_READ and MB_WRITE blocks in Structured Text read and write the world's other Modbus devices by unit ID over the plant network (function codes 1–6, a 5 ms round trip, DONE / BUSY / ERROR / STATUS with the device's exception code, or 11 when nothing answers).
- **Analog I/O on the Modbus module:** four 0–10 V inputs (AI1–AI4, input registers 1–4) and two 0–10 V outputs (AO1–AO2, holding registers 1–2), in millivolts through 12-bit converters; the inspector shows them.
- **Modbus RTU on a wire:** the remote I/O module serves its table by Modbus RTU on a 5 V TTL serial header when its bus is set to RTU (baud rate and frame format chosen): frames end with 3.5 characters of silence, CRC-16 checked, other addresses and broadcasts handled as the specification says, answers sent bit by bit on TX. A real Uno sketch acting as master writes coils, reads the inputs back through a cable, goes unanswered on a broken CRC and another address, and broadcasts.
- **Fix:** an Arduino's serial pin carried every frame as 8N1 whatever `Serial.begin` set: with 8E1, 8O1 or two stop bits the parity bit never reached the wire. The pin now sends the configured frame.

- **Enclosure check:** the DRC tab's Box switch defines the box the board goes into (inside sizes, the board's place, standoffs, lead length, margin, openings in the walls); the DRC then holds every part's body, top or bottom side, and the through-hole leads to its walls, floor and lid, naming the part and how far it goes in. Connectors leaving through an opening are allowed. Saved with the board.

- **Fabrication files checked against the board:** the Gerber ZIP is read back from disk by an independent RS-274X and Excellon reader (`Pcb/GerberReader.cs`), each layer painted at 20 µm and laid over the same layer painted from the board's geometry; differences are reported with where they are and their area, drill files hole for hole with plating and diameter. Wrong units, a flipped axis, a wrong aperture, an arc turning the wrong way or a missing object are caught before the files go to the maker.

- **Serial reception from the wire:** the Uno's USART receiver reads D0 as the circuit drives it, timed between the circuit's steps: start-bit check, three-sample majority per bit, 5–8 data bits, parity and stop bits from UCSR0B/C, frame (FE0), parity (UPE0) and overrun (DOR0) errors through a two-character buffer and the shift register. One board's TX wired to another's RX now carries what it prints. The transmitter sends the configured frame too (parity bit, two stop bits, 5–8 data bits); it always sent 8N1 before.

- **Line fault injection:** a Line Fault Injector (interference pulses to GND or VCC at random, or a line stuck at a level) and a Loose Contact (a wire that breaks now and then, or for good), seeded so a run repeats. They are switches in the circuit, so what a fault does depends on the driver and the receiver.

- **Pin drivers die of heat, not of a moment's current:** an Arduino pin burns when the power its driver dissipates, smoothed over 10 ms, reaches what 2.5 × its rated current would give: a dead short in a few milliseconds, never from a microsecond spike. Its over-current warning is given once per overload, not on every step.

- **Timeline tab in the debugger:** a logic analyzer inside the ATmega328P (`McuTrace`) records every pin's level changes and every interrupt's entry and RETI by cycle from the program's first instruction; the tab draws them as traces and handler bars, measures each pin's last period and high time and each interrupt's count and longest run, zooms with the wheel and scrolls back by dragging.

- **Registers tab in the debugger:** the ATmega328P's core (PC, SP, SREG flags, X/Y/Z, R0–R31, cycles) and its 75 peripheral registers by name and group, with the set bits named and 16-bit pairs combined; what changed since the last look is orange. Reading for display has none of a real read's side effects.

- **Arm deflection:** each robot axis has a torsional stiffness (its own, or estimated from its rating: about 350 N·m/rad per N·m for an industrial reducer, 6 for a hobby servo), and the cell panel shows how far the tool sags under the weight it holds, and how much of that the payload adds, at the arm's current pose.

- **Sensor sheets completed:** an HC-SR04 measurement jitters by 1.5 mm (σ), a compass has its 2 mG noise floor, and a GPS solution describes where the antenna was 80 ms before its output (stamped with that epoch), so a fusion filter that takes it as "now" lags a moving robot as on the bench. The world guide's sensor table gives each one's range, resolution, latency and noise.

- **Differential pairs and length matching:** the Nets tab holds differential pairs (found from their names: D+/D−, _P/_N, H/L) with their gap and largest length difference, and length-matched groups with a tolerance; each shows its routed lengths (tracks plus the via barrels they pass through), its difference and, for a pair, how much of it runs side by side at its gap. The DRC warns about a pair over its skew, a poorly coupled pair, and a group spread wider than its tolerance.

- **PCB manufacture and test checks (DFM/DFT):** with a switch in the DRC tab, the check adds what a fab and an assembly line look at: acid traps where tracks meet sharper than 90° (joins on pads and vias, filled by their copper, and overlapping tracks are not counted), solder-mask slivers under 0.1 mm, open vias in SMD pads, SMD pads within 2 mm of the edge, fine-pitch parts with no fiducials, two-pad parts with lopsided copper (tombstoning), silkscreen too small to print, too many drill sizes; and the nets a test probe can reach, with the test coverage and probes too close together.

- **Compiler version kept with the project:** every build records what it was made with in the board's code ("arduino-cli 1.6.0 · arduino:avr 1.8.8", shown on the build status); a later build with another compiler or core says so at the top of the compiler log.

- **Experiments:** **Simulation → Experiment: parameter sweep…** varies factors over a world (the environment, a part's parameter, an object's pose or mass, each over listed values or a range), runs every combination several times with its own seed in the background with the app's own solvers, and reduces metrics (an object's position, speed, path travelled, attitude, temperature, or a node's voltage) to one number per run: final, mean, min, max, RMS or range. Each combination gets its mean, standard deviation and 95 % confidence interval (Student's t); runs.csv, summary.csv, a report and the experiment with its seeds and the world as run are written for a repeat. `RobotWinStudio.exe --experiment spec.json` and `RobotWin.Headless --experiment` run the same without a window, and `robotwin.experiment` builds, runs and reads one from Python (a Jupyter example plots a sweep with its confidence intervals).

- **Arm path checking:** a cell robot tries each move along its whole path before starting it, sampled at 1° of joint travel: its link boxes, the tool and the part it carries against every other solid, the floor and its own links. A move that would collide is logged with what hits what and when (or, with the new *Collision check along the path: stop* setting, the arm faults before moving); contacts that exist when the move starts, like a box on the conveyor being picked, are not counted. It found the factory cell's own program swinging the box it had just lifted 150 mm into the conveyor's 80 mm end stop; the cell now lifts 250 mm.

- **Power report:** the modules with a known supply range (IMU, sonar, GPS, compass, LiDAR, camera, LED strip, servos, line and PIR sensors, LCDs) are watched on their own supply pins through a run: a brown-out goes to the events log when it happens and when it recovers (how long, how low), an over-voltage too, and when the world stops a report gives the power-up order, the brown-outs and the parts that never had enough. The sensor rover's LiDAR, fed from the Uno's USB 5 V, shows up just under the RPLIDAR A1's 4.9 V minimum.

- **Attitude fusion experiments:** gyro integration, a complementary filter, Mahony's and Madgwick's filters run side by side on an IMU's own readings (with a compass on the same body for heading) and are scored against the true attitude: tilt and heading error now and as RMS, on the IMU's card in the Sensors tab; with the log on, a CSV of every filter's estimate beside the truth. Over a minute of tumbling a 3 °/s gyro bias makes the gyro alone lose the tilt entirely while Mahony holds it within about 2° and learns the bias.

- **Temperature in the air and the sensors:** the world's ambient temperature sets its air's density, speed of sound and viscosity everywhere (the world runtime already did; the arena, the wings' Mach and Reynolds numbers and the ultrasonic sensors now follow it): an HC-SR04 echoes at 331 m/s at 0 °C and 355 m/s at 40 °C. An IMU's zero-rate and zero-g offsets and sensitivities move with its chip's temperature, each chip by its own coefficients drawn from the MPU-6050 sheet's spread; the chip warms 3 °C over its mount by its own heat in about a minute, so a gyro drifts after power-on and when a nearby driver heats the board. TEMP_OUT, the Sensors card and the CSV log (`chip_temp_c`) show it. A compass's sensitivity falls 0.3 %/°C; a line sensor follows its own temperature, not the air's.

- **Sensor data log:** the world's Sensors tab can record every reading of a run, one CSV per sensor: what the sensor measured beside the true value the simulation knows (an IMU's real specific force and rate, a GPS fix's real error, a line sensor's real floor reflectance) and a label column with the fault scenario active at that moment or `normal`. When the world stops the tables and a README are written to Documents\RobotWin\Sensor logs, labelled data for sensor fusion and fault detection.

- **Sensor failure scenarios:** a robot's sensors fail from a set time with Add feature → Fault scenario, as cell equipment already could: an IMU's gyro drift, vibration noise, frozen reading or silence on I²C (no ACK); a magnet near the compass or its silence; a GPS losing its sky (its NMEA goes on with no valid position) or pulled east by multipath; a LiDAR's dirty window or stalled motor; a line sensor's dirty lens or burnt-out IR LED. The firmware's reaction to bad readings can be tried before the real thing.

- **The model's limits:** a part's library page and its circuit inspector say what its simulation model leaves out or simplifies (no switching ripple in the averaged regulators, no contact bounce, a level 1 MOSFET, the averaged chopper of the stepper drivers …), for more than thirty models, so a result is read for what the model is.

- **Switching regulator modules:** LM2596, MP1584 and XL4015 step-down and MT3608 and XL6009 step-up modules, each with its 3D board. The model is the converter averaged over its switching period: the inductor current is a state that a voltage loop and an inner current loop drive, held by the switch's current limit, a buck's dropout or a boost's maximum duty, and kept from reversing by the diode. Below the under-voltage lockout it is off; turning on, its output rises over the soft-start while its capacitors charge. The input draws the output power plus the switch, inductor, diode and switching losses (an LM2596 from 12 V to 5 V at 1 A: about 85 %), which heat it up to its thermal shutdown.

- **Parameters and formulas in models:** a model's named values (genislik = 80, yukseklik = genislik / 2) are worked out in the order they need each other, with loops, unknown and twice-defined names reported. A part's dimension or a sketch's dimension typed as "=formula" is bound to them, marked ƒ, and follows every change; the sketches are solved again. Formulas take + − * / % ^, brackets, a decimal comma, pi and functions in degrees (sin, cos, atan2, sqrt, min, max, round, hypot …), on our own parser, which also replaces the number fields' old arithmetic.

- **Assembly mates:** the world's Align tool (H) picks a face, or a shaft's or wheel's axis, on two parts and offers the relations that fit: face to face, flush, concentric, parallel, perpendicular, distance and angle. They are solved together as CAD assemblies do: a fixed part stays put, the other moves as little as it can, parts screwed or grouped to it go along, and a dragged part takes its mated parts with it. The inspector lists a part's mates with their offsets, the freedom its own set has left, the mates that repeat others, and refuses one that contradicts them, naming those it fights. What the mates leave becomes the joint for the physics: a hinge on the axis, a slider along it, or a fixed joint. The pick is exact: the hit point counts the camera's near plane, and a face square to the part snaps onto its real face.

- **3D print check:** a printed part's inspector checks it for the chosen printer (Ender-3 V3 SE, Prusa MK4 and MINI+, Bambu Lab A1 and A1 mini, Voron 2.4): the build volume, a closed mesh, overhangs past 45° that need support, the thinnest wall measured through the part by rays (under two lines is flagged), the bed contact, and filament, time and layers estimated from perimeters, infill and support. It finds the way down with the least overhang and turns the part onto that face.

- **Dimensioned sketches:** a profile can be drawn as lines, circles and arcs held by relations (horizontal, vertical, parallel, perpendicular, equal, tangent, coincident, on a line or circle, midpoint, concentric, symmetric, fixed) and driving dimensions (length, distance, horizontal/vertical, diameter, radius, angle), all solved together as CAD sketchers do. A rough drawing settles onto its dimensions; the sketch says how many degrees of freedom are left or that it is fully constrained, names constraints that repeat others, and refuses one that contradicts them, naming those it fights. Its closed loops become the extrusion's outline and holes, and its dimensions can be changed from the inspector. A rectangle-and-round-holes profile turns into one with its dimensions.

- **DRV8825 and TMC2209 stepper drivers, and an Arduino Z axis:** the DRV8825 microsteps to 1/32 on its own 3.3 V logic (no VDD; VMOT 8.2–45 V); the TMC2209 standalone picks 1/8–1/64 with MS1/MS2 and spreads each step to 1/256 over the interval between STEP edges (MicroPlyer). Each carrier is drawn in its maker's colour. The built-in `z-axis-stepper` sketch drives an A4988 from an Uno (D2 DIR, D3 STEP, D8 EN) with a trapezoidal speed ramp, no libraries, and lifts a T8 carriage 40 mm and back.

- **Gear racks:** m1 steel and POM racks with straight-flanked 20° teeth. The assembler meshes a pinion whose pitch circle sits on the rack's pitch line: a sliding rack is driven by it, a fixed rack has the pinion roll along it carrying its carriage (a CNC gantry), each turn moving it by the pinion's circumference; the pinion is turned to start tooth in space.

- **Stepper motors and the A4988 driver:** a two-phase NEMA 17 (17HS4401, 17HS08) on its real equations (winding R and L, back-EMF and torque following the rotor's electrical angle, detent torque) and an A4988 that counts STEP edges, microsteps to 1/16 and regulates the phase currents as far as VMOT allows, with thermal shutdown. In a world the motor's torque turns a physics rotor, so a coupler, gear or pulley on its D-shaft (found by the assembler) and the whole mechanism load it: a square wave on STEP lifts a T8×8 carriage 8 mm a turn, and started too fast without a ramp it stalls. 3D models of the motor (caps, stack, pilot, M3 holes, connector, turning shaft) and the carrier (PCB, headers, heat sink, trimmer).

- **GT2 belts round two pulleys, and gears that start meshed:** a pulley's inspector fits a belt to another pulley in its plane: the belt's length from its spans and wraps, the closed belt to buy and how far to move a pulley to tension it, the shafts coupled at the ratio of the diameters. The scene draws the belt at GT2 sizes, its teeth in the first pulley's grooves and running with it. Meshing gears are turned by up to half a tooth pitch when assembled, so they start tooth in space rather than tooth on tooth.

- **Gears, pulleys, bearings, shafts and lead screws as real parts, assembled from where they are:** a Power transmission group with POM spur gears (involute teeth), GT2 pulleys, 608/688/625/6000 bearings, a KP08 pillow block, steel shafts, T8 lead screws with brass nuts, LM8UU linear bearings, collars and couplers, their 3D models generated from their dimensions and their masses from their materials. Placed as in a real machine, "Build the mechanism" (and starting the world) reads them: a shaft turns in the bearings it passes through, a gear is fixed on its shaft, gears whose pitch circles touch mesh, a nut drives the carriage it is bolted to. Parts nothing holds are named. Also: an overlap warning no longer fires for shapes whose boxes overlap but whose solids do not, meshing gears are no longer reported as sunk into each other, and new POM, brass and zinc materials.

- **Backlash and belt stretch:** a gear, rack, carriage or screw can have play: inside it the two sides move apart, at either flank the teeth push, so a reversing motor leaves the driven side standing until it has crossed the gap (1° at 0.1 rad/s: 175 ms). A belt can stretch under load with its span's stiffness, as a spring between the pulleys.

- **Bearings whose friction follows the load:** a hinge can turn in a ball or needle bearing, a bronze or plastic bushing or a pin in a printed hole, a slider can run on a linear bearing, a ball rail, V-slot wheels or a plain slide (inspector: Add feature → Bearing). The friction is μ × load × bore / 2 on a hinge and μ × the load across the rail on a slider, from the load the joint carries each step: a 10 kg flywheel coasts down 53 times faster on a bronze bushing than on a 608 ball bearing.

- **Springs and dampers in joints:** a hinge can have a torsion spring and a slider a coil spring of its own, with a damper, besides any motor (inspector: Add feature → Spring and damper, rates in N·mm/° and N/mm as catalogues give them). It is solved implicitly, so stiff springs stay stable: 1 kg on 1000 N/m sags 9.81 mm and swings at the 199 ms the formula gives, and a motor holding a lever against its return spring carries exactly k·θ.

- **Gears, belts and lead screws:** two joints of a world can be coupled by a gear pair, a belt, a rack and pinion, a belt-driven carriage or a lead screw (inspector: Add feature → Gear, belt, lead screw). It is a constraint in the physics solver, not a motor: the driven side's load comes back to the motor through it. The teeth's or thread's friction grows with the load (efficiency), and a lead screw's efficiency comes from its thread's angle: a T8×2 holds a vertical axis with the motor off, a T8×8 lets it down.

- **The Uno's UART on its TX pin:** the ATmega328P's USART now drives PD1 bit by bit (idle high, start bit, data, stop bit, at the baud rate the sketch set), so the line carries the real waveform: a scope decodes it and another device's input sees it. Tested with a real compiled sketch: what it prints is read back off D1's voltage with no errors.

- **Serial protocol decoder on the oscilloscope:** UART (baud, parity, framing and parity errors), I²C (START/repeated START/STOP, address and R/W, ACK/NACK, data) and SPI (modes 0–3, chip select) read off the probed wires at the record's logic threshold, drawn as a decode lane under the traces.

- **Manufacturers' SPICE models:** paste a `.model` card (continuations, comments, SPICE suffixes) on a diode, transistor or MOSFET and its numbers become the part's parameters in one undoable change; parameters our models do not use are listed. Validated with the 1N4148 card against Shockley's law.

- **AC analysis (frequency response):** small-signal response about the operating point, as SPICE's .AC: every device stamps itself at two backward-Euler step lengths, which separates the circuit into G + C/h, and (G + jωC)x = b is solved per frequency. The circuit page's new tab draws the Bode plot with the low-frequency gain, the −3 dB corner and the peak. Validated on an RC corner, an RLC resonance and a common-emitter stage.

- **HMI operator panel:** a dock tab of lamps, push buttons, switches, numbers, set points and bars bound to the PLCs' variables; while the world runs they show and write the program's values (inputs stay the pins'); saved with the world.

- **Station cycle time and bottleneck:** a PLC times its station's steps from its step variable (the CASE's): the current step, the cycles and their average, and the step that takes longest with its share of the cycle.

- **Ladder diagrams (IEC 61131-3 LD) for the PLC:** a visual editor with grid rungs (contacts, edges, comparisons, TON/TOF/TP/CTU, coils, set/reset, parallel branches by vertical links), checked as it is drawn and lit live while the world runs; a ladder runs by turning into Structured Text on the same interpreter. Tested: the seal-in latch, timers, a counter, edges and a comparison.

- **Fault scenarios for the cell's equipment:** a photoelectric sensor, limit switch, pneumatic cylinder or Modbus module can be given a fault that begins at a set time (dirty lens, stuck or dead output, welded contact, broken lever, seal leak, stuck valve, low supply, network down), played by the same physics, so a PLC program's reaction to it can be tried.

- **PLC running Structured Text (IEC 61131-3):** our own compiler and interpreter (BOOL/INT/DINT/UINT/WORD/REAL/TIME, TON/TOF/TP, R_TRIG/F_TRIG, CTU/CTD/CTUD, SR/RS, IF/CASE/FOR/WHILE/REPEAT, direct addresses, PLC integer wrapping, faults to STOP) in a new robot-cell part scanning its 8 inputs and 8 outputs every cycle; an editor that checks as you type, download into a running PLC, restart, and live variables. Tested end to end: a Modbus output says a part is there, the PLC's example program drives a pneumatic cylinder out and back by its end sensors and counts the part. A PLC can serve its %MW memory, inputs and outputs as a Modbus TCP server for a SCADA or HMI.

- **Pneumatic cylinder with a 5/2 solenoid valve:** a new robot-cell part whose two chambers are solved as masses of air (ISO 6358 valve flow, heating and cooling of the air, wall heat), pushing a piston rod body on a slider with seal friction; meter-out speed control, end sensors, and a coil that is a real load. Tested from a Modbus output: a Ø20 × 100 mm cylinder strokes in a few tenths of a second, fills to the supply pressure, pushes a wall with pressure × area, and a throttled exhaust slows it.

- **Photoelectric sensor and limit switch:** new robot-cell parts. A diffuse sensor's reach shortens with the target's reflectance (returned light ∝ reflectance / d²), a retro-reflective or through-beam sensor is cut by anything in its beam, both after their response time; a roller-lever limit switch changes over C–NO / C–NC past its pre-travel with half a millimetre of hysteresis, as real contacts in the circuit.

- **URDF mimic joints:** a `<mimic>` joint (a gripper's second finger) follows its master through a motor (multiplier × master + offset) instead of being driven separately; inverse kinematics and ROS commands leave it to its master. Tested: the mimicking finger stays within 0.5 mm of the commanded one.

- **Modbus TCP remote I/O:** a new robot-cell part with 8 digital inputs and 8 outputs on real circuit pins; while the world runs it is a Modbus TCP server (function codes 1–6, 15, 16, 23; by default 127.0.0.1:5020, unit 1) that a PLC, HMI or SCADA program reads and drives like a real module. Tested over TCP: a coil drives DO1, a cable takes it to DI3, the client reads it back.

- **ROS 2 mobile robots:** a robot free on the ground publishes `/odom` (`nav_msgs/Odometry`, its root frame from where it started) and `/tf` (odom → base_footprint) and follows `/cmd_vel` (`geometry_msgs/Twist`) on its drive wheels as a differential drive, with the track and wheel radius measured from its own shapes and a 0.5 s command timeout. URDF import: a `<ros2_control>` or `<transmission>` block decides which joints have motors and how they are commanded (a caster now turns freely), and a geometry-less root other than `world` (`base_footprint`) no longer bolts the robot down. Tested: a commanded rover drives 0.390 m in 2 s at 0.2 m/s and turns 84.8° in 1.5 s at 1 rad/s. The laser scanners and IMUs a URDF declares for Gazebo (`<sensor type="ray"|"gpu_lidar"|"imu">`, with rate, pose, scan, range, noise and the plugin's topic) are simulated from the world and published as `sensor_msgs/LaserScan` and `sensor_msgs/Imu`. `/joint_trajectory` (`trajectory_msgs/JointTrajectory`) moves position-driven joints through timed points (cubic between them) and holds the last.

- **ROS 2 connection through rosbridge:** a world connects to `rosbridge_server` (Environment → ROS 2): while it runs it publishes `/clock` (simulated time) and `/joint_states` (every hinge and slider by its URDF name), and takes `/joint_command` (`sensor_msgs/JointState`, as Isaac Sim's bridge does) as the joints' motor targets. No library: the .NET WebSocket client. Tested against a stand-in rosbridge: a commanded elbow reaches 0.500 rad and ROS hears it.

- **Inverse kinematics drives imported robots:** the arm kinematics panel now solves motorised joints (an imported URDF's axes) as it does servos: **Preview** shows the arm in the solved pose (hinges and sliders now preview too), **Make motor targets** keeps the solution as the motors' targets, and **Drive the axes** sends it while running; each row shows the motor's target. On the four-axis test arm a solved point is reached in the physics within 0.5 mm.

- **Robots from ROS: URDF and xacro import.** A `.urdf` or `.xacro` comes into a world with **Import 3D model or robot** or **File → Open**: every link with a body becomes a part at the zero configuration (its visual meshes in STL, OBJ, PLY or STEP, its collision meshes when a visual cannot be read, the materials' colours, the inertial mass), revolute and continuous joints become hinges and prismatic ones sliders with their limits, and every moving joint gets a motor as strong as the URDF's effort and as fast as its velocity, so an arm holds its pose against gravity; the inspector's new **Motor target** sends it elsewhere (hinges and sliders anywhere can have such a motor now). A `world` root bolts the base down; a robot on wheels stays free. `package://` meshes are found through the packages' folders or `package.xml` names. Xacro: properties, `${…}` expressions, macros with default and block parameters, includes, `if`/`unless`, `insert_block`, `arg`, `$(find)`. Not yet: `load_yaml`, DAE meshes; what could not be read is listed after the import.

### Changed

- **The 3D view leaves out what it cannot show:** the GPU renderer no longer draws objects wholly outside the view or smaller than half a pixel on screen (they still cast their shadows into it). The picture is the same; large scenes (terrain, the game view) spend less of the graphics card on what is not seen.

- **Cables in contact cost less to simulate:** a cable lying against parts is tested against fewer of them and more cheaply, with the same result: each part's own enclosing sphere (not one about its origin, which sits at a corner of many parts) settles the far ones, a convex part's extent along its faces is worked out once instead of over its corners at every test, and a move that starts farther from a part than it is long casts no ray. Measured with fully optimised code, a simulated second costs 38 % less in the motor glider, 34 % in the factory cell, 28 % in the PS5 plane, 21 % in the sensor rover and 18 % in the traffic light; worlds whose cables touch little gain little (line follower and quadcopter 5 %). The performance check now runs every world with the bodies' shapes the app uses, where cables turned out to be most of the cost (87 % in the factory cell); its baseline was recorded again. It now runs in a process whose code is fully optimised from the first call: with .NET's tiered compilation a world measured early in a run still ran partly unoptimised code, so its cost depended on the worlds measured before it (the car: 277 ms per simulated second alone, 103 after four others).

- **Cables cost a tenth of what they did to draw:** a running world no longer builds every cable's tube, plugs and materials again each frame. A cable keeps its models; its tube is refilled only when the rope moved (by more than 20 µm), the plugs are one shared box moved to the ends, and cables that look alike share one material. The cables' part of a frame went from 3.2–5.4 ms to 0.25–0.38 ms and the GPU no longer uploads ropes that did not move (0.8–1.3 → 0.5–0.8 ms); with more of the time left to it, the simulation kept up better too: line follower 0.72× → 0.84× real time, PS5 plane 0.25× → 0.37×, factory cell 0.70× → 0.79×.

- **Simulations run on their own thread:** a running world or circuit no longer advances inside the window's frames but on a thread of its own, beside the drawing. Heavy worlds keep up with real time much better and the window stays smooth: the factory cell went from 0.34× to 0.70× real time and from 79 to 144 frames a second, the ESP32 quadcopter from 0.70× to 0.93× (77 → 137 fps), the PS5 plane from 0.09× to 0.25× (71 → 133 fps), the line follower from 0.47× to 0.72× (51 → 163 fps). The pages, panels and instruments read a running simulation as before; breakpoints still stop the world at the program's exact instant. *Settings → Simulation and physics* can turn it off; `--bench` now writes how close to real time each world ran. The PCB's automatic routing and DRC (with its brief circuit run for the currents) work on a copy of the board in the background too: the board waits for them, the rest of the window does not.

- **Circuits with PWM and boards run faster:** the circuit solver no longer ends a time slice with a sliver of a step and climbs back from it in the next one, and after a discontinuity (a PWM edge, a switch) it restarts at a hundredth of the step it was going at instead of always at 0.1 µs. The line follower (two PWM motors, five line sensors, an Uno) needs 51 000 solver steps per simulated second instead of 64 000 and runs 18 % faster (867 → 715 ms of computing per simulated second); the ESP32 quadcopter 5 %. - **A liquid's wall field is summed only near the surface:** the distance field changes by at most the distance moved, so a node farther than the kernel's reach from a solid has none of it under its kernel, and one that deep inside has all of it. The result is exact.
- **The liquid solver does less per step and keeps its cores awake:** the whole water-glass pour (11 s of world, its ESP32 sketch tipping the glass) runs in 15.6 s instead of 28.2 s; while it pours, a 1 ms step takes 2.2 ms instead of 4.1 ms, and once the water is in the bowl the world runs almost in real time (1.1–1.2 ms a step). The results are the same to the last digit.
  - A team of solver threads stays awake between the passes of a step. Each pass over a few thousand particles takes tens of microseconds, and handing it to the thread pool woke its threads again every time, which cost as much as the pass. The workers now spin for up to 2 ms after a pass, take the next one at once, and sleep when the liquid stops. They take work in chunks from a shared counter, so the slower efficiency cores simply take fewer.
  - The stress analysis's matrix products (thousands per conjugate-gradient solve) and the wind tunnel's lattice steps run on the same team: the plate-with-hole stress check takes 1 s instead of 2 s.
  - Fewer passes over the particles: passes that touch only a particle's own data are done together (predict and walls; the final contact and the velocities; viscosity and surface tension share one neighbour loop), and each pressure iteration writes its correction to a second copy of the positions, keeping the walls up in the same pass. Each pass wakes every core, which at a few thousand particles costs about as much as the work.
  - The pressure correction reuses the kernel gradient its density pass computed at the same positions.
  - Each step gathers the shape, distance field and solid box of the bodies near the liquid once; a particle is checked against a body only when it is within the kernel's reach of that box in the body's own frame. A channel's long wall was checked by every particle across it, because its bounding sphere took them all in.
  - No buffers are allocated per substep (the neighbour grid and the viscosity's velocity copy are kept).
- **Concave bodies test only the pieces that can touch:** a glass or a tray is a set of convex pieces, and every piece of one body was tested against every piece of the other: two 25-piece bodies made 625 full tests a substep. Each piece now has a sphere about its own middle, and a pair is tested only when their spheres come within the contact margin; nothing farther apart can make a contact, so the contacts are the same. The glass world's bodies take 0.06 ms a step instead of 0.09 ms.
- **Pieces that share an edge no longer each press at the same point:** two wall pieces of a bowl side by side touch the table at their common corner, each with a contact of its own, so a bowl standing on a table made 120 contacts for 72 points. The same point with the same normal is one constraint; the deeper contact is kept. The rigid bodies of the water glass, its water pressing on them, take 0.80–0.84 ms a step instead of 1.05–1.14 ms (worlds solve contacts 16 + 4 times a substep).

Walls carry the liquid's pressure through density maps from each body's signed distance, and their reaction acts on the body: a glass carries its water's weight, a part in water feels buoyancy. No particle crosses a wall within a step, judged in the turning body's own frame. A liquid object is a box or cylinder the liquid fills when the world starts.
- **Density maps for every rigid body:** the share of the kernel a body's solid covers (Koschier & Bender) is precomputed on a grid in the body's frame, so the inside corner of a glass, its thin wall and a part behind it press on the liquid as they really are; flat analytic faces use the half-space formula, and a flat face behind a nearer one facing the same way adds no pressure.
- **Surface tension as cohesion, wetting by contact angle:** a pair attraction between particles whose strength follows from the liquid's real σ (Kirkwood–Buff: σ = (π n²/8)∫F(r)r⁴dr) holds sheets and streams together at any particle spacing; each wall pulls with Young–Dupré's work of adhesion σ(1 + cos θ), θ from its material (glass 25°, PLA 75°, PTFE 110° …), and carries the reaction. Replaces the continuum surface force, which only acted on resolved surfaces.
- **Air drag on spray:** exposed particles are dragged by the world's air and wind as spheres of their volume (Schiller–Naumann).
- **Anisotropic surface kernels** (Yu & Turk): each particle's kernel follows the shape of its neighbourhood, so a sheet one particle thick and a thin stream are drawn continuous; only particles with fewer than two neighbours are drops.
- **Renders iterate the pressure to a tolerance:** until no particle is compressed by more than 0.2 % ("Hassas", 40 000 particles) or 0.05 % ("Referans", 120 000), instead of a fixed number of iterations.
- **Liquid surface:** smoothed particles, a density field and surface nets give a closed surface with smooth normals; Fresnel reflectance per vertex (clear looking in, sky at grazing angles), a sun highlight, drops for loose particles. Glass and acrylic are drawn after the liquid, so water shows through a glass.
- **Glass and cups:** a "CAM" (soda-lime glass) material, and `tube.bottom` closes a hollow cylinder into a cup; the editor draws hollow tubes and cups.
- **Template "Su bardağı":** an ESP32 tips a glass of water held on a sideways SG90's horn and pours it into a bowl.
- **Renders compute liquids in full:** substeps no longer than the accuracy's physics step (every 0.1 ms on "Hassas"), no sleeping, 6000–30 000 particles and 5–12 pressure iterations by accuracy, recorded at 60 frames per second and replayed with a finer surface; falling drops are drawn with the streak they make between two frames. The live view solves about 3000 particles every 1 ms (a coarser step let a resting column compress by 8 %) and lets still water sleep.
- **A liquid starts with its exact mass, at rest:** a lattice that fills the whole volume (a whole number of layers in height), the mass set to ρ·V, then the liquid poured to rest beforehand (stepped under gravity with its motion damped, at the step it will run with), so the water stands still at its real density, in hydrostatic balance, when the world starts.
- **Open a saved render:** **Kayıtlı render'ı aç…** in the render window replays a `.rwanalysis` without computing it again.

- **Validation page:** **Yardım → 12. Doğrulama** (and the README) lists, for every solver, the theory or benchmark value, what the simulation gives, the deviation and the tolerance its test enforces.
- **Flow benchmarks:** the Schäfer–Turek DFG cylinder benchmarks 2D-1 (Re 20, steady) and 2D-2 (Re 100, periodic shedding) are tests now, along with sphere drag at Re 20 and Re 100 against the standard drag curve.
- **Trust notes on the 3D flow analysis:** each result states the real Reynolds number, cell size, blockage ratio, whether the boundary layer is resolved (y+), whether the force settled, and a grid uncertainty (GCI) from solving the first instant again on a coarser grid.

### Changed

- **CFD at the real Reynolds number:** the lattice-Boltzmann solvers use a regularized collision with Smagorinsky LES, stable down to τ → ½, instead of lowering the Reynolds number to 1000–1500 on the grid. An absorbing layer along the domain edges keeps pressure waves and eddies from reflecting back at the body.
- **Curved walls are second order:** where the true surface is known (from each part's signed distance), the wall sits at its real position on every lattice link (Bouzidi interpolated bounce-back) instead of a staircase of cells.
- **Inflow and far field:** the inlet is a velocity boundary that delivers the exact flow rate (it used to lose about 7 % in a channel), and the sides of the 3D domain are open at ambient pressure rather than held at the free-stream speed, which over-confined the flow. The 3D domain is wider (3.2 body sizes) and the run stops once the force settles.

### Fixed

- **Sphere drag was 30 % high** at Re 20; with the changes above it is within about 10 % of the standard drag curve, and the DFG cylinder drag is within 1 % of the benchmark.
- **ESP32 boards were idle in renders:** the render's runner had no sketch compiler, so an ESP32 world (the gamepad car, the plane, the traffic light) recorded its boards doing nothing; the render now compiles and runs the same firmware as the live world.
- **Help tables with many columns:** a wide table squeezed its last column to one letter per line; column widths are now normalized.
